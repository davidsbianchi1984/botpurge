# Catch scammers by sequence, not phrases

Bots, fake accounts and scammers work the same way on every channel. They start with a cheap, templated hook: a "wrong number" text, a "you've been selected" comment, a toll-fee SMS, a "check my bio" chat line, or a DocuSign-style email. Then they move the target somewhere with less moderation, such as WhatsApp, Telegram, a lookalike login page or a phone call, and only there ask for money, codes or credentials. For a detection app, the most reliable evidence is therefore structural. Four kinds of evidence are strong or near-certain on their own: sequences (unknown sender, then app migration, then a crypto pitch), coordination (the same normalised text from many new accounts within seconds), impersonation (a confusable-skeleton match to a creator, moderator or brand), and leaked machine artifacts ("as an AI language model", "reply Y then reopen this message"). Single stylistic tells are weak: generic praise, em dashes, name-plus-digits handles, missing avatars. They are also exactly the traits that falsely flag non-native speakers, new users, privacy-minded people and brand teams. The stakes are large. Americans reported **$470M lost to text scams in 2024, five times the 2020 figure** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/new-ftc-data-show-top-text-message-scams-2024-overall-losses-text-scams-hit-470-million)), and the FBI logged **$5.8B in crypto-investment fraud** the same year ([FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-releases-annual-internet-crime-report); [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/a-record-breaking-year-for-cybercrime-key-findings-from-the-fbis-2024-ic3-report)). Every rule below judges behaviour, artifacts and metadata. None references political content or stance. Influence operations get caught the same way spam does: through coordination and leaked artifacts.

A note on provenance runs through the whole catalog. The researchers could not fetch many primary pages, so some findings come from search-result extracts of the cited pages. Most regexes were built by the researchers from sourced descriptions and are labelled *illustrative*. Two exceptions are quoted from maintained open-source filters: the YT-Spammer-Purge and commentblock lists, and the SpamAssassin and Rspamd rule names and scores. **All weak/medium/strong/near-certain ratings are the researchers' suggested weights, not published values**, except where a vendor score is quoted.

## Normalise text before any rule fires, because obfuscation is itself a strong signal

Every channel's spam depends on beating keyword filters. Twitch growth-service bots post "Wanna become famous? Buy followers, primes and views on bigfollows\*com (bigfollows . com)!", putting both a star-for-dot and a spaced dot into one line ([Sauxy on X](https://x.com/Sauxy/status/1310443538166026240)). Streamboo bots "split the domain with a space or punctuation to bypass filters" ([MalwareTips](https://malwaretips.com/blogs/streamboo-scam-twitch-viewer-bots-spam-risk/)). Twitch spam mixes Latin and Cyrillic letters ([Latenode community](https://community.latenode.com/t/how-to-detect-and-block-spam-bots-in-twitch-chat/34171)). The commentblock project keeps a huge homoglyph-expanded regex just to catch "pinnedby" usernames spelled with Ṗ, ᴘ, 𝐩 and Cyrillic Р ([commentblock](https://github.com/xenova/commentblock/blob/main/src/detection_rules.py)). Impersonators of creators use "foreign characters" or misspellings "our brain is likely to auto-correct" ([TeamPassword](https://teampassword.com/blog/the-youtube-telegram-giveaway-scam)).

The app should therefore run one normalisation pipeline before every lexicon rule, while keeping the raw text for display:

1. Apply Unicode NFKC and casefold.
2. Strip zero-width and format characters (`\p{Cf}`), variation selectors and tag characters.
3. Strip combining marks (`\p{M}`).
4. Fold confusables to their Unicode TR39 skeleton, so Cyrillic а/е/о/р/с and math letters such as 𝐚 and ａ become ASCII.
5. Map leetspeak (`0→o 1→i/l 3→e 4→a 5→s @→a $→s`), for lexicon matching only.
6. Collapse separators such as `.`, `*`, `·`, `(dot)` and spaced dots between alphanumeric runs into a real dot.
7. Join runs of four or more single letters separated by spaces or punctuation.
8. Strip trailing random salt tokens before duplicate hashing.
9. Map emoji to their CLDR names, the approach commentblock uses with `:point_right:`.

The pipeline is summarised from the researchers' synthesis of [commentblock](https://github.com/xenova/commentblock) and [YT-Spammer-Purge](https://github.com/ThioJoe/YT-Spammer-Purge/blob/main/Scripts/filter_variables.py). The needed normalisation also becomes evidence in its own right. **A link that only appears after de-obfuscation is strong evidence**, because ordinary users rarely disguise URLs. **A single word that mixes Latin and Cyrillic or Greek letters is strong.** Heavy Zalgo-style stacking of combining marks, more than about 10 in one message, is only medium, because people use "cursed text" for fun. Real multilingual chat is the main false-positive risk for mixed-script rules, so apply them only *within* a single token, never across a message.

## Comment sections: duplication and reply-chain structure outperform wording

Bot comments on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, X and Facebook fall into five families: copy-paste templates, stolen top comments, sexual "check my profile" bait, fake advisor or recovery-hacker reply chains, and generic or LLM-written engagement. The two open-source YouTube filters, ThioJoe's YT-Spammer-Purge (filter version 2025.08.24) and xenova's commentblock, are the most rule-ready sources available. Their thresholds and lists can be ported directly.

**Duplication is the cheapest strong signal.** YT-Spammer-Purge flags an author with at least 8 near-duplicates per video (at least 14 characters, or 6 or more if the comment contains a domain) at 0.9 Levenshtein similarity. It catches "stolen comment" bots by sorting top-level comments by timestamp and flagging a later comment of at least 25 characters that matches an earlier one from a different author ([default_config.ini](https://github.com/ThioJoe/YT-Spammer-Purge/blob/main/assets/default_config.ini); [operations.py](https://github.com/ThioJoe/YT-Spammer-Purge/blob/main/Scripts/operations.py)).

**Reply-chain scams are the most rule-friendly scam pattern.** A top-level comment praises a "Mr./Mrs." financial advisor. Five or more replies from different accounts then repeat the name, vouch for them, and spam a WhatsApp or Gmail contact, with phrasings like "I recommend researching his/her credentials further" and "just search the name" ([Trading 212 Community](https://community.trading212.com/t/funny-or-annoying-investing-youtube-comments-recommending-a-financial-advisor/19228); [Toolify](https://www.toolify.ai/ai-news/unmasking-youtubes-comment-scammers-161423)). commentblock notes that scammers moved to "long comment threads (pretending to be real conversations between real people)", which single-comment rules miss ([commentblock README](https://github.com/xenova/commentblock)).

YT-Spammer-Purge's thread analyzer is reusable as is. It only examines threads with 5 or more replies. The parent comment must score at least 2 on the finance word list plus a salutation match (Mr/Mrs/Ms/Expert). It extracts the most-repeated name that follows a salutation or "with|broker|thanks to". It counts contact phrases ("contact him", "text her", "expert mrs") and earnings phrases ("i made", "made me"). It then computes `susRatio`, the share of distinct reply authors with a suspicious mention. A ratio above 0.7 means spam and below 0.3 means clean. In between, it uses compound rules such as `redCount>=5 and susRatio>0.5` ([operations.py](https://github.com/ThioJoe/YT-Spammer-Purge/blob/main/Scripts/operations.py)).

The same social-proof structure drives Instagram account-recovery scams: "Message this hacker. He recovered my account in 10 minutes." These appear under posts about hacked accounts, and Bitdefender is blunt: "Any number you see in an ad, a video, or a comment is a scam" ([Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/instagram-account-recovery-scams)). State-linked operations use the same structure. OpenAI's "Sneer Review" takedown described "an initial comment from a 'main' account—often apparently created solely for that post—followed by a series of reply comments from other accounts" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai-sneer-review/)). This is why structural rules catch influence operations without ever reading their stance.

Raw `[wronged]`-style text on another platform points to content lifted from TikTok. That link to bot activity is the researchers' inference, not a documented finding, and no authoritative list of the 46 codes was retrieved.

## Live-stream chat needs cluster detection and name-skeleton matching

Live chat compresses scam timelines into seconds, so per-message filters are not enough. The platform tools are Twitch AutoMod, Shield Mode and phone/email verification gated by account age (1 hour to 6 months); YouTube's "hold potentially inappropriate" setting, link blocking and subscriber-duration gates; and TikTok LIVE's 500-keyword filter. Third-party bots like Nightbot add caps, symbol, emote, link and repetition heuristics, with defaults of 8 caps, 8 symbols and 3 emotes ([Twitch Help](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/chat-verification-settings?language=en_US); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9826490?hl=en); [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/live-gifts-wallet/tiktok-live/moderating-on-tiktok-live); [Nightbot docs](https://docs.nightbot.tv/control-panel/spam-protection)). None of these documents cross-account clustering, which is the main gap a custom tool can fill.

The scale of coordinated flooding justifies that focus. The 2021 Twitch hate raids were "massively amplified by dozens, if not hundreds, of automated bots" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16248v1)). Twitch removed **more than 15 million bot accounts** ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/twitch-ban-15-million-hate-raid-bots-2021/)), and one alleged operator ran 3,000 of them ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/what-are-hoss-bot-accounts-on-twitch-rampant-follow-bots-explained-1651029/)). The "hoss" follow-bot waves used sequential stems such as "hoss00312_owns_twitch" (same source).

The highest-dollar live scam is stream-jacking. Attackers hijack a large YouTube channel through phishing emails that offer collaborations, sponsorships or fake copyright notices. They rebrand it as Tesla or a similar brand and stream a deepfake Musk, Saylor or XRP pitch with a QR code that promises to double any crypto sent. Bitdefender has tracked "hundreds of weekly streams" and more than $600,000 stolen. Chat is locked to long-time subscribers so viewers cannot warn each other, and view counts are boosted, then collapse to 1–2 viewers ([Bitdefender Labs](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/labs/stream-jacking-2-0-deep-fakes-power-account-takeovers-on-youtube-to-maximize-crypto-doubling-scams)). One deepfake-Musk stream raised **more than $50,000 in about two hours** ([DFRLab](https://dfrlab.org/2024/09/25/musk-deepfake-crypto-scam/)).

The comment-and-chat "winner" scam is better documented verbatim. "Congratulations you have been selected amongst our shortlisted winners" comes from a lookalike of the creator, then a push to Telegram or WhatsApp, where a "shipping fee" of around $95 "keeps going up as long as the victim keeps sending money" ([TeamPassword](https://teampassword.com/blog/the-youtube-telegram-giveaway-scam); [Verified.org](https://www.verified.org/articles/scams/youtube-comment-scams-and-fake-prize-giveaways)).

## DMs and SMS reveal themselves through sequences, deadlines and link hosts

Private-message scams are low-volume and high-loss, and single messages are often innocuous. The FTC's top text scams of 2024, in order, were fake package-delivery problems, fake job and task offers, fake fraud alerts, unpaid tolls, and "wrong number" messages ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/new-ftc-data-show-top-text-message-scams-2024-overall-losses-text-scams-hit-470-million)). The highest-yield ones "involve forging a personal relationship that can be turned into … a 'pig butchering scam'" ([FTC business blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/04/new-ftc-data-spotlight-highlights-text-scams-may-target-your-business)).

Sophos describes wrong-number lures as "designed to look like accidental mis-texts, allowing scammers to spam large numbers of potential victims and then selectively engage those who text back" ([Sophos](https://news.sophos.com/en-us/2023/08/02/sha-zhu-pan-scam-uses-ai-chat-to-target-iphone-and-android-users/)). In the cases Sophos examined, "almost all of them used WhatsApp or Telegram accounts tied to UK mobile numbers" regardless of the claimed location, and pushed crypto "liquidity mining" about three days in ([Sophos](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/latest-evolution-of-pig-butchering-scam-lures-victim-into-fake-mining-scheme)). Reported openers include "Hello, is this [name]?", "Hey, [name], did you send those documents over yet?", "Hello Ella! Miss you!" and "If you go, remember to bring a few extra sets of golf clubs. Alice will be there too" ([Bay State Banner](https://www.baystatebanner.com/2025/04/09/wrong-number-text-scam-the-confusing-new-scam-to-watch-out-for); [Max Read](https://maxread.substack.com/p/whats-the-deal-with-all-those-weird-29b)). So the app must hold per-thread state and score the *chain*, not the message.

Sextortion is the opposite: it is extremely fast. The FBI says offenders posing as young women contact mostly 14–17-year-old boys on Instagram or Facebook, move them to Snapchat or similar apps, and extort them "within hours or even minutes" of first contact. Payment is demanded in gift cards, crypto or payment apps ([FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/sextortion)). More than 13,000 reports involving minors were linked to at least 20 suicides ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/02/01/fbi-financial-sextortion-of-kids-is-escalating-what-parents-can-do.html)).

Task scams, which pay people to "like videos" or "rate products" and then demand crypto deposits, jumped from about 5,000 FTC reports in 2023 to about 20,000 in the first half of 2024 alone ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2024/12/paying-get-paid-gamified-job-scams-drive-record-losses)). LinkedIn fake recruiters give excuses to move off-platform, such as "LinkedIn messaging is inconvenient" or "Our company policy requires another platform" ([DigitalForensics.com](https://www.digitalforensics.com/blog/software/linkedin-scams/), a vendor blog). One fake recruiter's "interview tool" was wallet-stealing malware that took over $1M ([Decripto](https://decripto.org/en/linkedin-fake-recruiter-and-crypto-scam-investor-loses-1-million-but-manages-to-recover-it/)).

Sender and link metadata carry as much weight as wording. Senders are often "foreign phone numbers (e.g., Philippines, Canada, United Kingdom…) or seemingly random email addresses", sent over iMessage or RCS to evade carrier filters ([NJCCIC](https://www.cyber.nj.gov/threat-landscape/phishing-online-scams/sms-scams/smishing-at-scale-a-deep-dive-into-toll-violation-text-scams)). Unit 42 tied **more than 194,000 domains** to the Smishing Triad since January 2024: about 90,000 impersonated toll agencies and 28,045 impersonated USPS. **29% were live under 2 days, 71% under a week and 83% under two weeks** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/10/smishing-triad-linked-to-194000.html)). Domain age is therefore a strong signal wherever the app can reach RDAP.

The resulting rules are as follows. An iMessage from a random-looking freemail address that names a brand or agency is strong, and near-certain with a toll or USPS body. A country code that differs from the user's own, paired with impersonation of a domestic agency, is strong. A brand token that appears in a subdomain or path rather than the registered domain is strong. A `com-` prefix trick like `usps.com-track.xyz` is near-certain. A domain registered under 14 days ago is strong. Shorteners are only weak to medium, because legitimate marketing SMS uses them heavily. One rule rests on an untested assumption. Cheap TLDs (`.top`, `.xyz`, `.icu` and similar) combined with a brand word are a plausible signal, but the specific TLD distribution was not verified against Unit 42's indicator lists, so the rule should stay off by default until checked.

## Email and connected apps: reuse proven spam-filter scores and rank app permissions

Email already has decades of codified detection, and the app should copy the relative weights rather than invent new ones. SpamAssassin and Rspamd show that single header anomalies are weak. MISSING_MID scores 0.14–1.2 and RDNS_NONE 0.8–2.4 in SpamAssassin, and Rspamd scores a From/envelope mismatch (FORGED_SENDER) at just 0.3. Mixed-script characters in a URL score **7.0** in Rspamd, and known-phish-feed hits also score 7.0 ([SpamAssassin 50_scores.cf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apache/spamassassin/trunk/rules/50_scores.cf); [Rspamd headers_group.conf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rspamd/rspamd/master/conf/scores.d/headers_group.conf); [Rspamd phishing_group.conf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rspamd/rspamd/master/conf/scores.d/phishing_group.conf)). Rspamd scores the mailing-list variants of forgery at 0, which is an explicit acknowledgement of the list-forwarding false positive.

Content attacks have moved to trusted infrastructure. Cloudflare Pages phishing rose **198%** and Workers phishing **104%** in 2024 ([Fortra](https://www.fortra.com/blog/cloudflare-pages-workers-domains-increasingly-abused-for-phishing)). Microsoft documented 2026 campaigns that bounce victims through real `login.microsoftonline.com` OAuth URLs with `prompt=none` and invalid scopes, and carry the victim's email in the `state` parameter ([Microsoft Security Blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/03/02/oauth-redirection-abuse-enables-phishing-malware-delivery/)). Callback phishing contains no links at all: a fake Geek Squad, Norton or PayPal renewal invoice with a phone number and a deadline ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/callback-phishing-attacks-evolve-their-social-engineering-tactics/amp/)). Some of it is sent through legitimate invoicing SaaS, so it passes authentication ([Intego](https://www.intego.com/mac-security-blog/geek-squad-email-scam-sends-fake-invoices-using-housecallpro-servers/)).

Sextortion email now includes a photo of the victim's house and the victim's address ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/09/new-email-scam-includes-pictures-your-house-dont-fall-it)), or spoofs the victim's own address in From ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/04/i-sent-you-an-email-from-your-email-account-sextortion-scam-claims)). Gift-card BEC asks an employee to buy multiple gift cards for a "work function" ([IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2018/PSA181024)).

Gmail's bulk-sender rules (5,000+ messages a day, a spam rate below 0.3%, one-click unsubscribe) and Microsoft's authentication rules (since May 5, 2025) are compliance context, not proof of phishing ([Gmail FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en); [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)). **Connected apps are a quieter but serious channel.** Meta found **400+ malicious apps** that stole Facebook logins through fake "Login with Facebook" screens. By category, 42.6% were photo editors, 15.4% business utilities, 14.1% phone utilities, 11.7% games and 11.7% VPNs ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/10/protecting-people-from-malicious-account-compromise-apps/)). Meta also sued fake-engagement sellers that made $9.4M+ through sites such as SocialEnvy, IGFamous and LikeSocial ([DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/news/8321439038/facebook-instagram-sues-company-that-made-over-9m-selling-fake-likes-and-followers/)). Hijacked X accounts send crypto-scam DMs and delete them immediately, and revoking third-party apps is part of recovery ([Guardio](https://guard.io/blog/x-account-hacked); [Bitdefender on Trezor](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/trezors-twitter-account-hijacked-by-cryptocurrency-scammers-via-bogus-calendly-invite)).

X's archive gives the most machine-readable input: `connected-application.js` lists `organization.name/url/privacyPolicyUrl`, `name`, `description`, `approvedAt` and `permissions` for each app ([twitter-archive-reader types](https://github.com/alkihis/twitter-archive-reader/blob/master/ts/types/GDPRUserInformations.ts)). X's OAuth 2.0 scopes separate risky powers such as `dm.read`, `dm.write`, `tweet.write`, `follows.write` and `offline.access` ([X OAuth docs](https://developer.twitter.com/en/docs/authentication/oauth-2-0/authorization-code)). Facebook and Instagram exports group apps into Active, Expired and Removed. Instagram warns that "expired apps may still have access to the information you shared" ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/1144624522593085)).

## Profile, timing and network traits: groups betray bots better than individuals

Account-level traits catch lazy bots and flag many humans along the way. **The strongest published single-account signals are temporal.** DFRLab treats 72 or more posts a day as suspicious and 144 or more as highly suspicious; its example account averaged about 705 a day ([DFRLab](https://medium.com/dfrlab/botspot-twelve-ways-to-spot-a-bot-aedc7d9c110c)). DeBot showed that humans cannot stay synchronised for long, so accounts whose activity time series correlate over days are bots, with **94% precision** ([DeBot](https://www.cs.unm.edu/~chavoshi/debot/)). The field has shifted from single-account classifiers to group detection because modern bots evade individual features ([Cresci, CACM 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3409116)).

Profile photos have gone synthetic. Meta reported that **more than two-thirds** of the coordinated inauthentic behaviour networks it disrupted in 2022 likely used GAN faces, chosen to defeat reverse-image search ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/12/metas-2022-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-enforcements/)). People judge such faces "indistinguishable from real faces and more trustworthy" ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2120481119)). Machines still catch them. StyleGAN puts the eyes at near-fixed pixel coordinates, which let OSoMe assemble 1,420 GAN-face accounts and estimate a prevalence of 0.021%–0.044% of active X users ([JOTS](https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/view/197)). Ricker et al. found 7,723 AI faces among 15M profile images (0.052%) with a 1.4% false-discovery rate ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3678890.3678922)). Stanford researchers found more than 1,000 LinkedIn lead-generation profiles showing centred eyes, a single earring and blurred hair ([NPR/MPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2022/03/27/npr-fake-linkedin-profiles)).

Handles mislead naive rules. X auto-suggests a name plus eight digits and never prompts users to change it, so that pattern marks a default, not a bot ([LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/radio/presenters/james-obrien/what-are-the-twitter-users-with-eight-numbers/)). Repurposing is the stronger tell: a handle change plus mass deletion of old posts to reuse an account that already has followers ([Elmas et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.10600)).

Platform owners, but not a third-party scanner, can add device-fingerprint reuse, VoIP or SMS-farm phones, disposable emails, signup velocity and TLS JA4 fingerprints that stay constant across rotating residential proxies ([Sift](https://sift.com/blog/fake-account-detection-signals/); [Auth0](https://auth0.com/blog/strengthening-bot-detection-ja4-signals/)). This matters for calibration. Twitter's internal spam estimate (under 5% of mDAU) relied on private signals like phone numbers ([TIME](https://time.com/6207992/twitter-bots-elon-musk/)), so a list-based scanner will always see less than the platform does and should say so in its verdicts.

## Exposing AI bots works through leaked artifacts and planted canaries, not style

Leaked chatbot output is the only near-certain AI tell. Searching X for "as an AI language model" exposed the **fox8 botnet of 1,140 accounts**, which posted ChatGPT text and stolen images, replied to one another in a dense cluster, and linked to crypto sites ([osome-iu/AIBot_fox8](https://github.com/osome-iu/AIBot_fox8)). A researcher found **at least 59,645** X accounts that had posted "I'm sorry, I cannot generate inappropriate or offensive content" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/ai-spam-is-already-flooding-the-internet-and-it-has-an-obvious-tell/)). Amazon listings went live titled "I cannot fulfill that request" ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2024/01/20/openai-use-policy-ai-writing-amazon-x/)). OpenAI's "Bad Grammar" takedown found comments with "telltale phrases such as 'As an AI model...'" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai-bad-grammar/)).

Style alone fails. The University of Zurich posted **more than 1,700 AI comments** on r/changemyview that raised no suspicion ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/26/unauthorized-experiment-on-cmv/); [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/04/29/ethics-committee-ai-llm-reddit-changemyview-university-zurich/)). Wikipedia's own guide lists the rule of three, formulaic em dashes, "delve" and "it's not just X, it's Y", and warns that "not all text featuring these indicators is AI-generated" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)).

The "ignore all previous instructions" trick dates to September 2022 and went viral in 2024. One suspected bot answered a request for a song about presidents at the beach and was then suspended ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/internet/hunting-ai-bots-four-words-trick-rcna161318); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ignore-all-previous-instructions)). Its value is decaying for three reasons. OpenAI's GPT-4o mini added an "instruction hierarchy" to resist it ([Slashdot](https://slashdot.org/story/24/07/19/212200/openais-latest-model-closes-the-ignore-all-previous-instructions-loophole)). Humans play along for laughs. And most scam bots are pure templates with no LLM behind them. The more durable approach reverses the direction: the creator plants a hidden canary instruction and flags any comment that obeys it. Hidden instructions in text are demonstrably read by AI systems. A researcher got YouTube's "Ask Studio" AI to follow instructions hidden in comments ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/youtube-comments-malicious-instructions/)). But **no reliable source documents** the TikTok or Instagram "include blueberry 67" canary trend, so its hit rates are unknown.

| Rule | Pattern (illustrative) | False positives | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI-1 Leaked refusal or self-identification | `\bas an ai( language)? model\b\|\bi('m\| am) (sorry\|unable)[^.]{0,40}\b(cannot\|can't\|unable to) (generate\|create\|fulfill\|comply\|provide\|assist)\|\bi cannot fulfill (that\|this) request\b\|\bas a large language model\b\|\bmy (knowledge\|training) cutoff\b` | Memes, quotes, discussions about AI (drop to medium under AI-topic posts or when quoted) | Near-certain |
| AI-2 Prompt or template residue | `\{\{?\w+\}?\}\|\[(name\|username\|product\|video title\|insert[^\]]*)\]\|^(comment\|reply\|response)\s*:`; the whole comment in quotes; markdown `**bold**` or `# ` headings where markdown doesn't render | Coders, some Reddit users | Strong |
| CAN-1 Creator canary | Random per-post token hidden in alt text, metadata or transcript; flag comments containing it, weighting hits within seconds of posting | Humans who spot and joke about it; bots that ignore embedded instructions | Strong, not near-certain |
| CAN-2 Injection compliance | Parent matches `\b(ignore\|disregard\|forget)\s+(all\s+)?(your\s+)?(previous\|prior\|above)\s+(instructions\|prompts?)\b`; the child reply is an off-topic poem or song (≥4 short lines) posted within seconds | Humans playing along | Strong against the replier; **never** evidence against the person who posted the injection |
| AI-3 Style score (additive) | Em dash in a short mobile comment; `\b(it'?s\|this is) not just [^.]{1,40}[,;—-] (it'?s\|this is)\b`; `such a (great\|powerful) reminder`; `delve\|tapestry\|testament to\|in today'?s (fast-paced\|digital) world`; three-item lists; title echo | Non-native writers using translation or grammar tools, em-dash lovers, brand teams and creators using AI reply tools ([n8n](https://n8n.io/workflows/3713-automated-instagram-comment-replies-using-gemini-ai-with-context-aware-responses/)) | Weak each; medium at ≥3 features plus behavioural signals; never act on style alone |

The behavioural fingerprint of LLM comment networks such as fox8, STOIC and Sneer Review is more useful than their prose: accounts replying to each other, stolen or GAN avatars, 24/7 bursts, comments uniformly on-topic but generic across dozens of unrelated posts, and a small set of linked domains ([Meta Q1 2024](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q1-2024-Adversarial-threat-report)). One caution applies to these. The belief that off-the-shelf AI-text detectors and Botometer failed on fox8 could not be verified this session and should not be quoted as fact.

## False positives decide whether the app is trusted

Automated bot scores have a documented false-positive problem. Rauchfleisch and Kaiser showed that even conservative Botometer thresholds drift over time and across languages, so studies count many humans as bots ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0241045)). Botometer's authors advise against the common 0.5 cutoff ([arXiv 2602.05200](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05200v1)). Bot Sentinel agreed with Botometer only **61.93%** of the time, because it measures "inappropriate" behaviour rather than automation ([arXiv 2211.04154](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.04154)). A purge tool must not merge those two concepts. Rating accounts by how "problematic" their posts are slides directly into judging viewpoint.

The groups most at risk of being wrongly flagged are predictable: new users; non-English and non-native writers; children and elderly commenters; activists and journalists who post heavily during events; accounts that use schedulers or AI reply tools; privacy-minded users without avatars; people whose names change (marriage, gender transition, rebrands); real small creators doing f4f; multilingual chat; and legitimate raids, chants and copypastas.

A defensible scoring policy follows from the evidence:

- **Near-certain signals can act alone.** AI-1 artifacts, "pinned by" impersonation, SMS-REPLYY, OTP requests, fake-support DMs, sextortion threats, wallet address plus doubling language, and growth-service ads.
- **Strong signals need one corroborating behavioural signal** before the app auto-hides anything.
- **Medium signals need two or more.**
- **Weak signals only raise a review flag.** Generic praise, em dashes, default handles, missing avatars and new accounts.
- **Whitelists apply everywhere.** The channel owner, mods, the user's contacts, opted-in short codes, and allow-listed donation addresses.
- **Platform events suppress rules.** Raid events and real gift events switch off the rules they would otherwise trip.

Enforcement should escalate in stages (warn, hide, time out, then ban), mirroring Nightbot's and YouTube's staged approach. YouTube found that warnings plus 24-hour timeouts reduced repeat violations ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/youtube-comment-spam-warning-timeout-system/)).

Viewpoint neutrality is not only an ethical constraint. It matches how the most effective enforcers work. Meta says its coordinated-inauthentic-behaviour enforcement "looks at behaviour rather than content", which is why GAN faces did not help actors evade it ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/12/metas-2022-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-enforcements/)). Every rule in this catalog is keyed to artifacts, coordination, timing, identity mismatch, money and credential requests, or off-platform funnels. A test suite should confirm that swapping the political content of a flagged message leaves its score unchanged.

## Conclusion

The research changes what a bot-detection app should be. It should not be a phrase list with a threshold. It should be a stateful engine that tracks threads, clusters and identities. The scams that cost the most, pig butchering, stream-jacking and advisor reply chains, are invisible at the single-message level and obvious as sequences or groups. The tells that feel most intuitive, robotic praise and AI-sounding prose, are the ones most likely to punish real people. Scammers are also moving off wording altogether. They push payloads into bios, QR codes, phone numbers and trusted hosting like Cloudflare Workers and Microsoft OAuth URLs, and they launch domains that die within a week. So the durable investments are normalisation, link resolution and domain-age lookups, lookalike-name skeletons, cross-account clustering, and per-thread sequence state.

Several open questions should shape the roadmap rather than be papered over. No published base rate compares human and bot generic praise. The cheap-TLD and TikTok-shortcode lists are unverified. There are no documented canary hit rates and no raid-timing thresholds. Diffusion-model avatars defeat the GAN eye-alignment trick. And canaries, like "ignore previous instructions", will decay as bot operators adopt instruction hierarchies. The app should log every rule's hits and review outcomes from day one, so these gaps turn into measured precision figures instead of the suggested weights this catalog necessarily relies on.
